Anthropic/AI slowdown overhang — partial rebound Tuesday: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's weekend call for slowing frontier AI development (echoed by Altman and Musk) triggered a broad chip selloff on Monday (SOX -~6%, Asian tech -2.4%). Tuesday saw a partial US recovery, but Taiwan names continued to bleed as the TWE session digested Monday's US move. Strategists broadly called the selloff a temporary setback with no evidence of actual capex cuts — 79% of BofA fund managers surveyed do not expect any AI company to cut capex in 2026. (1) (2)

Oracle earnings — cloud beat, margin miss, capex balloon: Oracle reported FQ1 cloud infrastructure revenue of $7.4B (+121% YoY), beating estimates, with RPO at $664B. However, gross margin shrank to 61% from 66.3% prior quarter, and capex tripled to $28.5B in the quarter (FY27 guide: $90–95B). Oracle also disclosed expanded job cuts and a new Ellison share-sale plan. Price targets fell an average of 4.9% post-earnings. This is a read-across for hyperscaler capex intensity — positive for the supply chain, but the margin compression spooked ORCL holders. (3) (4)

US-Mexico AI hardware export rules: The US government is reportedly pressuring Mexico to accept new rules of origin for AI hardware exports, limiting non-North American components in chips and servers, to prevent Chinese circumvention of tariffs. This is a potential supply-chain disruption for Taiwan/Asian ODMs and component makers with US-bound shipments. (5)

FOMC meeting Wednesday — rates overhang: Bond traders have piled into bearish positions ahead of Wednesday's FOMC meeting. US 10-year yield near 4.99%, WTI ~$105/bbl. Rate-hike fears are compounding the AI slowdown narrative as a dual headwind for high-multiple tech names. (6)

Hyperscaler capex remains structurally intact: Despite the slowdown rhetoric, Anthropic itself contracted 14.8GW of compute capacity (~$517B in commitments) through August. Oracle booked >$30B of new AI cloud contracts in Q1. Customers are signing multiyear chip deals — "that's not what the front end of a downturn looks like," per one analyst. (7) (8) (9)

Taiwan ASIC/substrate names hit hardest: Alchip (-6.5%), Global Unichip (-5.7%), Elite Material (-4.3%), and AURAS (-3.4%) led TW declines. These names have the highest beta to AI ASIC and advanced packaging demand and were the most exposed to Monday's US selloff flowing through to Tuesday's TWE session. No company-specific catalysts identified for most; the move appears to be prior-session beta catch-down.
