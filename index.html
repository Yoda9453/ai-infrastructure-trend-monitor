Step 3 — Sector / Tape Context (covering multiple names simultaneously)

The macro trigger — AI slowdown calls: Over the weekend of 13–14 Sep, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay urging the industry to "slow the pace" of frontier model development; OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk publicly backed the call. This was the single dominant catalyst hitting virtually every name on this list on Mon 14 Sep. (1) (2)

Demand-destruction fear for AI infrastructure capex: The market's read-through was that a deceleration in model development reduces near-term demand for compute, memory (HBM, NAND), optical interconnect, power equipment, and advanced packaging — hitting the entire AI infra supply chain simultaneously. A UBS basket of AI data-center stocks closed down ~6.2% in Europe; a European semi basket closed down ~7.6%. (3)

Rates headwind compounding the selloff: The US 10-year Treasury yield briefly crossed 5% for the first time since 2023 on Mon 14 Sep, adding a valuation discount to high-multiple AI names across the list. (4) (5)

Power / data-center infrastructure names hit hardest among industrials: Vertiv, Eaton, and Schneider Electric — the three power-infrastructure plays on the list — all fell 6–8%, as investors repriced hyperscaler capex expectations. The move was flagged as the worst for Eaton in 19 months. (6) (7) (8)

Optical networking cluster (GLW, COHR, LITE, FN, 300308 CH, 300502 CH): Optical names were disproportionately hit — both because of the sector-wide AI capex fear and because Corning's company-specific $2B equity deal amplified the read-across to the entire fiber/transceiver chain. (9) (10)

Hyperscalers diverged from infra names: GOOGL (+3.2%), META (+2.7%), MSFT (+2.0%), and AMZN (−1.3%) significantly outperformed the semiconductor and infrastructure names — consistent with the "AI doomsday trade" thesis that a slowdown in model development benefits hyperscalers (less capex burn) while hurting suppliers.
