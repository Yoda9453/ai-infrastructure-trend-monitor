Step 3 — Sector / Tape Context

Meta Muse = the tape catalyst. Meta's personal AI agent Muse hit #1 on the US Apple App Store over the weekend. Truist analysts estimated Muse could add $28.5B in incremental revenue by 2030. The read-across: agentic workloads are CPU-intensive, directly lifting AMD, Intel, and ARM. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gained 4.3% on Mon 21 Sep, its fifth straight session of gains. (1) (2) (3)

AMD crosses $1 trillion market cap. AMD became the 14th US company and 7th semiconductor firm to cross $1T in market value intraday on Mon 21 Sep, closing at ~$615. OpenAI reportedly agreed to deploy 6 GW of AMD Instinct GPUs across multiple generations; Oracle separately committed to AMD capacity. (4) (5)

Optical D2D interconnect showcase at ECOC 2026. Lumentum, Qualcomm, and Corning jointly announced a demonstration of high-density 1060nm VCSEL optical die-to-die connectivity for AI scale-up systems at ECOC 2026 in Malaga, Spain (Mon 21 Sep). This single announcement lifted all three names and provided a sector read-across to optical networking peers. (6) (7)

Neocloud IPO pipeline expanding. Nscale (UK-based CoreWeave/Nebius rival) filed an S-1 targeting ~$3B raise at ~$30B valuation. Separately, Australian data center operator Firmus Grid filed for a $5B+ IPO. Both signal continued investor appetite for AI infrastructure equity. (8) (9)

Data center power regulation tightening. California Gov. Newsom signed SB 886 and AB 2383 on Mon 21 Sep, requiring data centers to report energy/water use and barring them from passing electricity costs to residents. The EU separately confirmed plans for mandatory minimum energy performance standards for data centers in Q2 2027. Near-term cost headwind for hyperscalers and data center operators; potential tailwind for power management and cooling names. (10) (11)

Taiwan PCB/substrate names rallied in sympathy. ITEQ, Taiwan Union Technology, Unimicron, and Compeq all moved on the broad AI infrastructure re-rating. T. Rowe Price was reported to be ramping bets on Greater China and Taiwan AI supply chain names — PCBs, chip equipment, power components — as a catch-up trade to global AI capex. (12)
