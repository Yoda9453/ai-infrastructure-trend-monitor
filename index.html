Daily News

Broad AI infrastructure rebound, Sep 17: Chip and memory stocks staged a sharp recovery on Sep 17, reversing a bruising selloff earlier in the week triggered by AI safety/slowdown concerns (Anthropic CEO essay, AI leader calls for slower development). The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index added ~3.1–3.2% on the day; XSD +4.5%. The Fed's 25bp rate hike on Sep 17 was absorbed without lasting damage. (1) (2)

Memory price surge / Intel CEO warning: Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan warned publicly that memory prices have surged over 500% and the shortage could worsen into 2027. This was the primary catalyst for MU (+5.5%), SNDK (+6.2%), SIMO (+5.0%), and read-across to SK Hynix (000660 KS, -0.8% — muted, possibly capped by the Intel/SK Hynix JV denial). (3) (4)

Intel / SK Hynix US memory JV speculation: Reuters reported Intel and SK Hynix were in early talks about a potential memory manufacturing partnership at Intel's Ohio fabs. SK Hynix denied any finalized plans on Sep 17. The story drove INTC higher on Sep 16–17 and kept 000660 KS in focus, though SK Hynix's denial capped its gains. (5) (6) (7)

Nvidia GPU price inflation / hyperscaler capex read-through: Bloomberg Intelligence noted that rising Nvidia GPU prices signal strong demand and improve cloud economics for Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet, supporting further hyperscaler capex. This underpinned the broad AI infrastructure rally and benefited data center names (DLR, EQIX), power names (ETN, SU FP, SIE GY), and networking names (MRVL, ANET, COHR). (8)

CCL / substrate sector sell-off (Taiwan): Elite Material (2383 TT, -7.67%) and Taiwan Union Technology (6274 TT, -6.71%) both saw volume spike to 3x their 20-day averages on Sep 17 with heavy selling pressure. Doosan (Korea) announced a KRW 968bn CCL capacity expansion — the largest in its history — which may have raised supply concerns for incumbent CCL producers. Both names had been rated Overweight by Morgan Stanley on Sep 14 with aggressive price targets, suggesting the sell-off may reflect profit-taking after a strong run (Elite Material +184% YTD, Taiwan Union +163% YTD). (9) (10) (11)

Data center power / US legislative action: The US House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3 on Sep 17, directing states and utilities to consider requiring data centers to pay for their own power infrastructure costs. This is a potential headwind for hyperscaler capex economics but was not visibly negative for power infrastructure names (ETN, SU FP, SIE GY all rose), suggesting the market read it as neutral-to-positive for power equipment suppliers. Oracle separately signed 433MW and 568MW renewable energy PPAs with RWE and ENGIE for its Texas data centers. (12) (13)
